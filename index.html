Firm Assistant

01

n8n · RAG · Supabase / PostgreSQL

Give your team a place to ask questions about company documents and database records. Firm Assistant connects those sources in one workflow, with routing and checks around how it handles each request.

More about this project

Company answers often sit in separate documents and database tables. Finding one can mean searching through files or asking someone to pull the right record.

In this personal project, I built a workflow that takes a question, chooses whether to search documents or read the database, and returns an answer in one place. It logs requests, checks its answers against test questions, and blocks requests it should not handle. Database queries are limited to reading data.

The point is to make routine questions easier to handle while keeping a way to check where an answer came from. A team would still need to review the workflow and its access rules before using it with real company data.

Sales Analysis

02

Excel · Data quality · Profitability

I checked the data first, then used Excel to compare product margins and see what deep discounts did to profit.

More about this project

Can we trust the numbers?

The project started with about 113,000 sales records and a simple question: which products make money, and how do discounts affect profit?

Before answering that, I noticed repeating patterns that didn’t look right. Comparing the rows revealed fabricated copies of genuine records with changed values. Almost half the dataset was affected.

I flagged those records and kept them out of the calculations instead of deleting them. This kept the original data available so someone else could review the checks and follow the analysis.

Discounts were quietly eating into profit

After separating the reliable data, I found that profit margins were 51% on full-price sales, compared with 31% on deeply discounted sales. Selling more through discounts did not necessarily mean making better returns.

Helmets also stood out, with a margin of around 59%. The more expensive products weren’t always the ones with the strongest margins.

What this helps a business decide

The dashboard helps stakeholders see which products offer stronger margins and where discounts deserve a closer look. It gives them a clearer starting point for pricing and product decisions.

I built the checks, calculations and interactive dashboard in Excel using formulas, so the results can be traced back to the underlying records.

Research & Report Automation

03

n8n · APIs · JavaScript · PDF reports

Start with a topic and turn the research into a report. This workflow gathers web sources and creates a formatted PDF with citations and charts, ready for you to review and share.

More about this project

A useful research report takes more than a quick web search. Someone has to gather sources, compare what they say, make charts when needed, and put everything into a document people can read.

I built a workflow that handles those steps from a topic prompt. It passes source material through research, analysis and writing stages, then creates a PDF with citations and optional charts. It keeps source links with the material and checks quotations against the original text.

I tested the report with and without charts. A quotation check rejected two lines in one test; I have not confirmed why. The final PDF still needs a human review. The workflow helps with the repetitive gathering and formatting, while leaving the judgment about the findings to a person.

E-commerce Sales Analysis

04

SQL · Power BI · Sales performance

I used SQL and Power BI to find what drove falling sales, where cancelled orders created risk, and which areas still grew.

More about this project

Sales were falling—but where was the problem?

I analyzed over 131,000 sales records to understand why revenue was falling, how much potential revenue was tied to cancelled and returned orders, and where the business still had room to grow.

I cleaned the data in SQL, removed duplicates and empty records, and checked the dashboard calculations against the SQL results. I then built a two-page Power BI dashboard and a stakeholder report with suggested actions.

One category explained most of the decline

Revenue fell each month across the three-month period. One category accounted for about 81% of that decline, making it the clearest place to start investigating.

The business also depended heavily on a small part of its range: three categories generated 91% of revenue. That meant a slowdown in one major category could have a large effect on total sales.

Cancellations and returns added up

About INR 8.3 million in potential revenue was tied to cancelled and returned or lost orders. This was money associated with orders that did not become completed sales—not money recovered through the project.

I also found regions with higher cancellation rates, helping stakeholders narrow down where to investigate delivery, order handling or customer issues.

There were growth opportunities too

While most categories declined, one clothing category grew by 27%. Business customers also placed higher-value orders and cancelled less often than individual customers.

These findings gave stakeholders areas worth exploring alongside the problems: support the growing category, understand what business customers need, and investigate the biggest sources of lost sales.

What this helps a business decide

The dashboard shows where sales are falling, where cancellations are concentrated and which parts of the business are growing. The report connects those findings to practical next steps.

This project measures revenue and order performance. Cost data wasn’t available, so it does not measure profit.
