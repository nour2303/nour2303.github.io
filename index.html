Sales Analysis

01

Excel · Sales data · Profit

I checked the sales records, then used Excel to see which products kept more profit and how discounts affected it.

More about this project

Can we trust the numbers?

I started with about 113,000 sales records to find which products earned more profit and how discounts affected it.

First, I found rows that copied real sales but changed the numbers. Almost half the data was affected.

I marked those rows and left them out of the calculations. I kept them in the file so another person could check my work.

What discounts showed

Full price sales had a 51% profit margin. Sales with discounts above 15% had a 31% margin. That makes large discounts worth a closer look.

Helmets had a margin of about 59%. A higher price did not always mean a higher profit margin.

Why it matters

The Excel report shows which products kept more profit and where discounts reduced it. That gives a business a place to start when reviewing prices.

I built the checks and report in Excel. The numbers can be traced back to the sales records.

Firm Assistant

02

n8n · Company files · Database

Firm Assistant brings company files and database records together, so a team can ask a question in one place. It checks where to look before answering.

More about this project

Company answers can be spread across files and databases. Staff may have to search several places or ask someone else to find a record.

In this personal project, I built a workflow that decides where to look, reads the right source and gives an answer. It records each request, checks answers with sample questions and refuses requests it should not handle. It can read database records but cannot change them.

The aim is to make routine questions easier to handle. Before using it with real company data, a team would need to check the answers and who can access each source.

Online Sales Analysis

03

SQL · Power BI · Sales trends

I used SQL and Power BI to see why sales fell, where cancelled orders were a problem and which products still grew.

More about this project

Where were sales falling?

I looked at over 131,000 sales records to find out why sales fell, which orders did not complete and where the business still had room to grow.

I removed repeated and empty entries with SQL. I then checked that the numbers in my Power BI report matched the cleaned data.

One product group stood out

Sales fell each month. One product group made up about 81% of the drop, making it the first place to investigate.

Three product groups made up 91% of sales value. A slowdown in one of them could have a big effect on the whole business.

Cancelled and returned orders

Some orders were cancelled or returned before becoming completed sales. I also found locations with more cancelled orders. That gives the business a clearer place to look for delivery or order problems.

There was growth too

One clothing group grew by 27% while most groups fell. Business customers also placed larger orders and cancelled less often than individual customers.

These findings point to useful next steps: look into the biggest drop, check why orders are cancelled and learn from the groups that are growing.

Why it matters

The Power BI report shows where sales fell, where orders were cancelled and which parts of the business grew.

Cost data was not available, so this project does not measure profit.

AI Voice Receptionist

05

Calls · Appointments · Staff handoff

I designed a voice receptionist that can answer common questions, check appointments and pass tricky calls to a person. The goal is to free up staff time while keeping bookings reliable.

More about this project

Small service businesses often spend staff time answering the same questions and checking the calendar. I designed steps for routine answers, appointment checks, call summaries and passing a caller to a person when needed.

The receptionist should only confirm a booking after the calendar saves it. If information is missing, the calendar fails or a person cannot take the call, it should take a message instead of making up an answer.

The test plan includes 10 to 15 types of calls, such as booking conflicts, repeat requests, calendar failures and callers changing their minds. Time and cost savings have not been measured.
